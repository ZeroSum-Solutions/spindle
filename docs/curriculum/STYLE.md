# Curriculum style

How Spindle lessons read. It applies to the base curriculum and is the starting point that gyms and coach-creators adapt for their own method and modality courses. Safety wording is in [SAFETY.md](SAFETY.md); where the two differ, SAFETY.md wins.

We studied how strength, rehabilitation, hypertrophy and movement coaches teach in public articles and videos: their structure, tone and cueing. We took patterns only. No text, image, diagram or program from anyone else is in Spindle, and no lesson is branded with or endorsed by a coach.

## Voice

- **Coach to coach.** Write to a working trainer between sessions: direct, warm, no lecture tone.
- **Plain words first, the term second.** "The ball-and-socket joint (glenohumeral joint)". Keep the proper term: trainers meet it with clients and clinicians.
- **Short sentences, active voice.** One idea per sentence.
- **Calm about pain.** Most shoulder pain settles. We name warning signs clearly and never use fear to sell an exercise.
- **No hype.** No "fix", "bulletproof", "forever", "the only exercise you need", "never do X". Say what the evidence supports and for whom.

## Lesson shape

Every lesson ends on the gym floor: an assessment, a cue, an exercise choice or a referral.

1. **One idea up front.** Open with the single idea the lesson serves, in one or two sentences.
2. **Sections with one job each:**
   - *What it is*: the structure or concept.
   - *Why it matters*: what it changes for the client.
   - *How to see it*: what to look for in a set-up, a rep or a screen.
   - *What to do*: a cue, an exercise choice, a progression, or a referral.
3. **Admit the simplification.** When a model is simpler than the body, say so in one line and say what is left out.
4. **Stop and refer, where the risk is.** Each assessment and progression carries its own short warning (SAFETY.md › Where warnings sit).
5. **Close with a check.** A short self-check or scenario, and what the lesson does *not* prove.

Lessons are short: 8 to 15 minutes of reading, chunked under headings. A longer topic becomes two lessons.

## Screens and fixes

- **Screen, then act, then re-check.** Give a clear start position, what to look for, and what a difference between sides or reps might suggest. After a drill, re-check: the honest test is whether anything changed.
- **A screen finds where to look.** It never diagnoses and never clears anyone.
- **Order the options.** When several things can help, give them in a fixed, teachable order, from least to most demanding, and say when to stop.
- **Offer an easier version.** "If this is too much, use this instead" is part of every drill.

## Cues

- **Few and external.** One to three cues per drill. Prefer a direction or a target ("reach the hands toward the wall", "push the floor away") over a muscle command.
- **Define a cue before you use it.** Many cues mean two things. Say which one you mean and what the lifter should feel or see.
- **Cues are practice-based.** A cue is coaching experience unless an approved claim supports a specific effect. Label it that way.
- **Watch cues under load.** A cue that works with a light bar can turn into something else under a heavy one. Say when to change it.

## Evidence

- **Two labels, block by block:** *Science* (bound to an approved claim with sources) and *Practice-based* (coaching experience). The label is never implied by tone.
- **Confident where the research is strong.** Plain statements, with the claim one tap away.
- **Honest where it is not.** "Coaches often use…", "In practice…". No made-up percentages or study counts.
- **Contested frameworks are practice context only.** Models such as fascial lines, tensegrity of the whole body, or posture-as-cause stories may appear as clearly labelled practice-based context with a note that the evidence is limited. They never carry a Science label and never explain a cause as fact.
- **A video is never a source.** If a video presents research, cite the paper.

## Visuals

- **A visual next to every movement concept:** an original diagram slot or a short, optional video segment.
- Diagrams are original SVG with a title and a description. Until one exists, the block says it is not available.
- Videos follow the embed rules in the phase 3 plan: click to load, practice-based label, never required for completion.

## Quizzes

- Graded questions test science and bind an approved claim.
- Scenario questions test the trainer's action: continue, adjust, stop, or refer (and which tier).
- Wrong answers are plausible mistakes a trainer could make, never jokes or diagnoses.

## Anti-patterns

- Clickbait headings and profanity.
- Absolute rules ("never press overhead").
- One cue for every situation ("shoulders down and back").
- Posture described as damage.
- Causal chains with no testable prediction ("the shoulder is just the symptom").
- Product plugs or course links inside a lesson.
- Copied phrases, diagrams, programs or exercise sequences from anyone else.

## For coaches who build on Spindle

Method and modality courses use the same block structure and labels. A gym's method is always practice-based, in its own words, under its own name. The base supplies the science; the coach supplies the system.
