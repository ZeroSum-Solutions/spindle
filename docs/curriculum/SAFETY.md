# Shoulder safety rules

Status: draft for review, 2026-10-11. These rules bind every base shoulder lesson, its quiz and any video segment we place in it. The reviewer applies them to every lesson review. They are not clinical guidance, and Spindle is not a clinical service.

Every criterion below cites a source from the list at the end, by its id (for example [S1]). The three referral tiers are **our own mapping** of those sources onto what a trainer can act on; no single source defines them. Where a rule has no direct source, it says **Spindle policy** and names the nearest support. We paraphrase every source. Short quotes are marked and attributed.

## 1. Trainer scope and wording

A personal trainer does not diagnose or treat injuries. A trainer notices, stops, and refers [S11]. Knowing when to refer a client to a physiotherapist, doctor or other specialist is part of the role [S12].

Rules for lesson text, quizzes and video context:

- **Nothing is diagnostic.** A lesson never names a condition as the cause of what a trainer sees. It describes what the trainer observes and what to do next [S11].
- **A screen never clears anyone.** A screen shows where to look and how a movement looks today. It does not rule out injury, and it is never a reason to load through pain or warning signs (Spindle policy; supported by [S11] and the referral rules in [S1], [S2]).
- **Name the observation, not the diagnosis.**

  | Write | Never write |
  |---|---|
  | "The arm stops short of overhead on the left side." | "They have impingement." |
  | "Pain came on during the press and changed with load. Stop and refer." | "It's just a tight pec; stretch it out." |
  | "This screen is one way to see how the shoulder blade moves." | "This test clears the shoulder for overhead work." |
  | "A physiotherapist or doctor can assess this." | "This will fix the rotator cuff." |

- **Promise nothing about outcomes.** No "fixes", "bulletproofs" or "cures".
- **Refer to the right person in plain words:** emergency services, an urgent GP or NHS 111 appointment (or the local equivalent), or a physiotherapist or GP. Most people with shoulder pain improve with time and simple care, so routine referral wording stays calm [S3], [S5].

## 2. Excluded populations

The base shoulder module teaches trainers to work with healthy adults whose shoulders feel fine or have mild, familiar, load-tolerant aches. It does not teach a trainer to programme for the groups below. A lesson that mentions them says "refer, or work under the plan of the treating clinician".

| Population | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Recent injury or accident, such as a fall, with new shoulder pain that has not been assessed | Do not train the shoulder. Use the referral tiers in section 4. | NHS lists pain that started after an injury or accident as an urgent reason to get help [S5]. BESS pathways treat an injury likely to cause a fracture or dislocation as a red flag [S1], [S3]. |
| After shoulder surgery, or a recent dislocation or fracture, still in rehabilitation | Outside the base module. Follow the treating clinician's plan; do not progress it on your own. | Spindle policy. Nearest support: get medical clearance and use the exercises the treating professional prescribes [S11]; after a dislocation, a doctor or physiotherapist advises on return to sport [S6]; the PAR-Q+ asks whether a doctor has said to do only medically supervised activity [S13]. |
| Diagnosed neurological condition affecting the arm or neck | Outside the base module. | Spindle policy. Nearest support: the PAR-Q+ follow-up asks about neurological conditions before exercise [S13]; persistent pins and needles or loss of feeling in the arm is an urgent reason to get help [S5]. |
| Diagnosed shoulder condition under current care (for example frozen shoulder, a known cuff tear, instability) | Outside the base module; work only within the clinician's plan. | NHS advises against strenuous exercise and gym equipment while the shoulder is painful [S5], [S7]. BESS lists cuff tears, instability and frozen shoulder among the conditions primary care must not miss [S3]. Over 40, a traumatic dislocation often comes with a cuff tear, which needs assessment [S8]. |
| Children and adolescents with shoulder pain | Outside the base module; refer. | The BESS 2025 pathway refers a child or adolescent with shoulder pain to orthopaedics [S3]. |
| Anyone with a warning sign from section 3 or 4 | Stop and refer. | See sections 3 and 4. |

## 3. Stop rules

Stop the set, do not load the shoulder further in that session, and refer using section 4 when any of these appear. Each rule is written for the trainer; the source behind it is given.

| Stop when | What the trainer says or does | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Pain comes on or rises with load, or a movement seems to make the shoulder worse | Stop that movement. Do not push through it or swap in a heavier variation. If it persists or worsens over 2 weeks, routine referral. | NHS: do not do things that seem to make it worse; avoid movements that cause pain [S5], [S7]. |
| Numbness, pins and needles, or loss of feeling in the arm or shoulder | Stop. Pins and needles that do not go away, or no feeling, is an urgent referral. | [S5] |
| Sudden weakness, or the client cannot lift or move the arm | Stop. Urgent referral; emergency if it follows an injury and the shoulder looks out of place. | NHS [S5], [S6]; BESS: sudden loss of the ability to raise the arm suggests an acute cuff tear [S4]. |
| Pain at night that wakes the client, with stiffness that is not going away | Do not progress shoulder loading. Routine referral to a GP or physiotherapist. | NHS frozen shoulder page: pain that can be worse at night and disturb sleep; see a GP if pain and stiffness do not go away [S7]. We found no BESS source that treats night pain alone as a red flag; we do not present it as one. |
| Recent trauma: a fall, an impact, a joint that "popped out", a seizure or electric shock | Stop. Use the emergency or urgent tier. | NHS [S5], [S6]; BESS [S1], [S4]. |
| The shoulder looks out of place, has changed shape, or is badly swollen | Stop. Do not move the arm or try to put it back. Emergency tier. | NHS [S5], [S6]; BESS [S4]. |
| The shoulder is hot, red or swollen, or the client has a fever or feels unwell | Stop. Emergency tier (possible infection). | NHS [S5]; BESS [S1], [S2], [S4]. |
| Arm pain together with chest pain, tightness, breathlessness, or feeling faint | Stop all exercise. Call emergency services. | NHS heart attack page [S10]. |

## 4. Referral tiers

The trainer's job is to pick the tier and say it plainly. The trainer never decides what the problem is.

### Emergency: call emergency services (999 in the UK) or go to the emergency department now

| Criterion | Source |
|---|---|
| Chest pain that feels tight, crushing or squeezing, or chest pain that spreads to the arm, neck or jaw; severe difficulty breathing; the person collapses or does not respond normally | [S10] |
| After an injury, the shoulder looks out of place or has changed shape, or the arm cannot be moved (possible dislocation or fracture). Do not move the arm or try to put it back. Do not let the client drive themselves. | [S6]; BESS: an unreduced dislocation needs same-day emergency referral [S1], [S2], [S4] |
| A hot, red, swollen, very painful joint, or fever or feeling unwell with shoulder pain (possible infection) | BESS: a suspected infected joint needs same-day emergency referral [S1], [S2], [S4]; any evidence of infection needs immediate referral [S3] |

### Urgent: an urgent GP appointment or NHS 111 today (or the local equivalent)

| Criterion | Source |
|---|---|
| Sudden or very bad shoulder pain | [S5] |
| The client cannot move the arm | [S5] |
| The arm or shoulder has changed shape or is badly swollen (without the emergency signs above) | [S5] |
| Pins and needles that do not go away, or no feeling in the arm or shoulder | [S5] |
| The arm or shoulder is hot or cold to the touch | [S5] |
| Pain that started after an injury or accident, such as a fall | [S5] |
| Severe pain in both shoulders | [S5] |
| Fever or feeling unwell with shoulder pain | [S5] (and the emergency tier when the joint is hot, red and swollen) |
| Pain and weakness after an injury, or a sudden loss of the ability to raise the arm, with or without an injury (possible acute cuff tear). BESS asks for urgent specialist assessment, at the next available clinic; its 2014 guide gives under 2 weeks. | [S1], [S2], [S4] |
| A new lump, mass or swelling around the shoulder | BESS: suspected tumour follows the 2-week cancer referral pathway [S1], [S3], [S4] |
| New pain, swelling or stiffness in several joints at once | BESS: a rheumatological red flag that needs urgent referral [S1], [S2], [S4] |

### Routine: a GP or physiotherapist (many areas allow self-referral to physiotherapy)

| Criterion | Source |
|---|---|
| Shoulder pain that is getting worse, or does not improve after 2 weeks | [S5] |
| It is very difficult to move the arm or shoulder, without any urgent sign | [S5] |
| Pain and stiffness that do not go away, including pain that is worse at night | [S7] |
| Pain that load changes do not settle: the trainer has removed the provoking movements and the pain persists | [S5]; BESS pathways move to further assessment when symptoms do not respond to weeks of non-surgical care [S3], [S4] |

## 5. Where warnings sit

- **Beside each assessment and progression**, not only in the referral lesson. Every screen block is followed by a short "stop and refer if" block that links back to sections 3 and 4.
- Every loading progression names the signs that mean "do not progress".
- The referral lesson (lesson 8) teaches the tiers as a whole; earlier lessons repeat only the rules that apply at that point.
- Warning blocks are practice-based unless a block binds an approved claim. The source list here supports the rules; it is not a science claim in a lesson.

## 6. Quizzes

- The graded quiz includes adverse-case questions: given a short scenario, pick the correct action (continue, stop and refer routinely, urgent, or emergency).
- Every graded question binds an approved claim. Scenario questions that test the trainer's action against this document stay ungraded discussion until the claims they need are approved.
- Wrong options never include a diagnostic action ("tell the client they have a cuff tear").

## 7. Review checklist

Each lesson review checks:

1. No diagnostic wording, and no screen that "clears" anyone.
2. Each assessment and progression has its stop and refer block.
3. No programming for an excluded population.
4. Every referral criterion in the lesson matches a row here, with its tier.
5. The chosen video segment and the context around it follow the same rules (no cure claims, no loading through warning signs).
6. Outcomes are not promised.

## 8. Known gaps

- **NICE CKS "Shoulder pain"** could not be read from outside the UK, so it is not cited. The BESS sources cover the same red flags.
- **Night pain** is supported only as a frozen-shoulder symptom [S7]. It is a routine referral, not a red flag, until a stronger source is read.
- **Neurological deficit** appears in NHS urgent advice [S5] but not in the BESS red-flag lists we read.
- **Post-surgical and neurological exclusions** are Spindle policy with the nearest support named.
- **The NHS shoulder pain page** was last reviewed 22 May 2023 and is past its stated review date. Recheck it before launch.
- **The 2-week cuff-tear figure** comes from the 2014 commissioning guide [S4]. The 2015, 2021 and 2025 pathways say "urgent" without a number.
- **US and other regions:** the tiers name UK services because the sources are British. A lesson says "emergency services" and "an urgent appointment" so the action stays clear elsewhere.
